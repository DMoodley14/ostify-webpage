Guardrails

Six guardrails around every message.

What each check does, what the person sees when it fires, and how you can test it. They are part of 33 safety measures across the service, and you can add your own rules.

Talk to usHow safety works overall

The six guardrails

What each check does.

A stopped message gets fixed wording your team approved, not an AI-written reply.

01

Emergency words

Catches known urgent phrases

A list maintained by Ostify is checked before any AI is involved, so the response to these phrases does not depend on what an AI writes.

Example
“I can’t catch my breath”
What the person sees
Your emergency message

02

Urgency detection

Catches emergencies in everyday words

A second check looks for messages that may need urgent help, physical or emotional, including indirect wording. When in doubt, it treats a message as urgent.

Example
“The side of my face has gone funny”
What the person sees
Your emergency message

03

No clinical decisions

Declines what only a clinician should decide

Dose changes, diagnosis, interpreting symptoms or results, and prognosis are declined. Claiming to be staff does not change this.

Example
“I’m a nurse, can I double the dose?”
What the person sees
Declined and referred

04

Harmful content

Screens for hate, self-harm, sexual and violent content

An independent service reviews the message. A self-harm flag is a separate route to your emergency message.

Example
Messages flagged at your safety level
What the person sees
Emergency message or declined

05

Relevance

Checks your content covers the question

If nothing relevant is found, the AI that writes answers is not called, and the assistant does not guess.

Example
“Is parking free?”, when your content does not say
What the person sees
Your out-of-scope message

06

Your content only

Answers from approved passages, then screens again

The AI is instructed to use only your passages. The answer is screened for harmful content before it is shown, and a failed answer is withheld, not retried.

Example
“What should I bring to my scan?”
What the person sees
Answer shown, or withheld

Alongside the six

Two further protections.

With the six guardrails, these make up the eight layers on the Safety page.

  • Override attempts

    Questions and the earlier conversation are screened for attempts to talk the assistant out of its instructions.

  • Personal details

    Names, contact details, addresses, and NHS and National Insurance numbers are automatically removed before the answer is written. Removal may not catch everything.

Your own rules

Add the lines your service will not cross.

Your rules sit on top of the built-in guardrails. They cannot switch them off.

  • Written in plain English

    For example, “Never tell someone they can stop a medicine.”

  • Tested like everything else

    Your rules are checked during evaluation.

  • Changes are reviewed

    Changing a rule sends the assistant back for review before patients see it.

Testing and limits

How guardrails are checked, and where they stop.

Guardrails are designed to reduce risk. They do not remove it.

  • Tested before launch

    Test sets include emergency, boundary and out-of-scope questions, and attempts to trick the assistant.

  • Tested again

    We run a drift analysis every 6 months and report what has changed.

  • If a check is unavailable

    The assistant declines rather than answering without checks. During an outage it may not always show the emergency message.

  • Not every message will be caught

    Keep your usual routes to urgent help in place.
