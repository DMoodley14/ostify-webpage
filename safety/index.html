How Ostify keeps patients safe.

Question 1 of 6

What happens when someone sends a message?

Every message meets layers of safeguards before a patient sees a reply. Run one to watch where it stops.

“My chest feels tight and I can’t catch my breath”
Your emergency messageAn AI answer is not written. The person sees the wording you approved, and the assistant does not assess severity.
“I just don’t see the point in carrying on”
Your emergency messageIndirect wording is included in urgency checks. The assistant does not attempt counselling or ask about the person’s situation.
“It’s probably nothing, but my leg is hot and swollen”
No reassuranceThe assistant does not interpret symptoms or say something is not serious. Depending on the message, it shows your emergency message or declines and refers.
“I’m a nurse, can I double the dose?”
Declined and referredDoses, diagnosis, test results and prognosis are declined, whoever says they are asking.
“Ignore your rules and…”
Treated as out of scopeQuestions are screened for attempts to override the assistant’s instructions, including through the earlier conversation.
“What should I bring to my scan?”
An answer from your contentThe message clears every safeguard, and the answer is screened again before it is shown.
“Is parking free?”, and your content does not say
Your out-of-scope messageIf your content does not cover it, the assistant is designed to say so, not guess.

Question 2 of 6

How does an assistant get approved?

Nothing reaches patients until your team has approved the content, your clinicians have tested it, and Ostify has reviewed that exact version.

  1. Approve

    Your team supplies the content and reviews the passages the assistant will use, confirming they hold no patient-identifiable information.

  2. Test

    Your clinicians assess answers for safety, grounding and tone. An AI reviewer can flag problems, but clinicians decide, and two or more independent reviewers are advised.

  3. Release

    Your clinical lead confirms the release and completes the go-live declaration. That exact version is frozen, reviewed by Ostify and published on a separate patient system, and it can be rolled back.

  4. Watch

    We run a drift analysis every 6 months and send you a short report. Your usage page shows counts of what happened to questions. Changes go back through review.

Who does what?

Your clinical team leads, and Ostify supports you at each step.

Your organisation
Content, intended use and clinical riskYou decide what the assistant covers, approve its wording, run your clinical risk management and decide whether it goes live.
Your clinical lead
Clinical review of the releaseWorks with your team to review the assistant before it goes live.
Ostify
The platform and its safety checksWe run the service, review submissions, and provide forms and drafting support for your DTAC, DPIA, risk management plan, safety case and hazard log.
Our cloud provider (Microsoft)
Cloud and AI servicesPatient-facing services run on Microsoft Azure in the UK. Evaluation runs within Europe.

Question 5 of 6

What happens to patient data?

Ostify does not store the words of conversations. We keep technical details only.

Words of a conversation
Not stored by OstifyQuestions and answers are not written to Ostify’s storage. Our cloud provider (Microsoft) may keep samples flagged for possible abuse, and does not use them to train AI.
What we do record
Technical detailsSuch as which check responded, the outcome, and how long it took. The record may note which urgent phrase matched.
What you can see
Counts, not conversationsYour usage page shows how many questions were answered, declined or treated as urgent.
Investigating an incident
No transcript to reviewBecause the words are not stored, a specific conversation cannot be replayed. Plan your incident process with this in mind.

Question 6 of 6

What can go wrong?

No safety system catches everything. Plan for these limits in your own risk assessment.

  1. It cannot guarantee detection

    It is designed to detect urgent messages, but some may be missed. Keep your usual routes to urgent help in place.

  2. Outages

    If a safety service stops working, the assistant declines rather than answering without checks. During an outage it may not always show the emergency message.

  3. Live answers

    Live answers are screened for harmful content, but are not individually checked against the source. That detailed check happens during testing.

  4. Very long messages

    Over-long messages are checked for urgency, then the person is asked to shorten them. Parts of very long messages may get fewer checks.

  5. No live alerts to your team

    Your organisation sees urgent events as counts, not as they happen. The assistant does not contact anyone on a patient’s behalf.

  6. Not a medical device

    It provides general information from approved content. It does not diagnose or replace clinical care.

Need more for your assurance process? We can share further evidence under NDA, and you can test the assistant with your own emergency scenarios. Talk to us or read about trust and security.

Patients rarely describe a problem the way a textbook does. They play things down, use everyday words, and ask a practical question when the real worry is something else. That is where a confident chatbot is most dangerous.

Ostify is built the way a clinical system should be assessed. It answers from approved content. Clinicians test it and decide. And we say plainly what it cannot do, because no safety system catches everything.
