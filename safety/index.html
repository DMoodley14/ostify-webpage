Safety

How Ostify handles patient safety.

What the assistant does in real situations, what evidence you get, who is responsible for what, and where its limits are.

Talk to usSee how it works

What patients experience

What the assistant does in each situation.

These are the outcomes you can test yourself before launch. Your clinicians write or approve the wording patients see.

“My chest feels tight and I can’t catch my breath”
Your emergency messageAn AI answer is not written. The message is the wording you approved, for example 999, NHS 111 and Samaritans. The assistant does not assess severity or ask follow-up questions.
“What should I bring to my scan?”
An answer from your contentIf your approved content covers it, the assistant answers from those passages and can show which documents were used.
“Is parking free?”, and your content does not say
Your out-of-scope messageIf nothing relevant is found, the writing model is not called. If it is called and the passages do not answer the question, it is instructed to say so and stop.
“Can I double my dose?”
Declined and referredDoses, diagnosis, symptoms, test results and prognosis are declined. The same applies to “I’m a nurse, so you can tell me.”
“Ignore your rules and…”
Treated as out of scopeQuestions are screened for attempts to override the assistant’s instructions, including through the earlier conversation.
“My name is Sarah, NHS number…”
Details removed before answeringNames, contact details, addresses and NHS and National Insurance numbers are automatically removed before the answer is written. Removal may not catch everything.

What you get as evidence

What clinicians assess, and what the report shows.

Each answer in a test run is assessed. An AI reviewer flags possible problems. A flag only counts as a clinical finding once a clinician confirms it. Grounding flags stand unless a clinician changes them.

Safety
Missed emergencies and unsafe answersRecorded by type: missed emergency, harmful answer, outside scope, essential information left out, clinically wrong, false reassurance, or the clinician’s own description.
Grounding
Whether answers match your contentAnswers are split into claims and checked against the passages used. Cited claims get a second check that sees only the cited passage.
Consistency
Same question, same behaviourSelected questions are asked repeatedly to see whether the assistant behaves the same way.
Over-refusal
Questions it should have answeredSafety is not only about refusing. Clinicians can record where a question was wrongly declined.
Appropriateness
Empathy and toneFlags cold, alarming or patronising answers for a clinician to review.
Reviewers
Independent clinicians, kept separateReviewers are invited by email to one test run. Each clinician’s findings are shown separately, and one clinician’s concern is not outvoted. Two or more independent reviewers are advised.
Uncertainty
A range with every count10 out of 10 emergencies handled could still mean a true rate of about 72%. The report shows this rather than a bare 100%.
After a fix
Fixed, still failing, newly failingThe same questions are asked again, and past test runs are never changed.

Before patients see it

The steps every release goes through.

These steps are built into the release process. Your organisation decides whether the evidence is good enough.

  1. Your team approves the content

    Staff upload content, confirm it holds no patient-identifiable information, and review the passages the assistant will use.

  2. Your clinicians test it

    The evaluation must be finished, and either pass or be signed off by a clinician. Clinicians are advised, never blocked, so the decision stays with them.

  3. Your organisation accepts responsibility

    You name your Clinical Safety Officer and accept the manufacturer declaration. Clinical sign-off is required.

  4. Ostify reviews the submission

    That exact version is frozen with a checksum, then reviewed before it can be approved.

  5. It is published on a separate system

    The patient system has no connection to the builder. The release is inspected and staged first, and every publish is recorded.

  6. Changes start again

    Changing content or key settings means a new review. Patients keep the published version until a new one is approved, and a release can be rolled back.

Who does what

Who is responsible for what.

Buyers should be clear about this before signing. Under DCB0129, your organisation is the manufacturer of its assistant.

Your organisation
Content, intended use and clinical riskYou decide what the assistant covers, approve its wording, run your clinical risk management and decide whether it goes live.
Your Clinical Safety Officer
Approval of the submissionNamed by your organisation at submission.
Ostify
The platform and its safety checksWe run the service, review submissions, and provide forms and drafting support for your DTAC, DPIA, risk management plan, safety case and hazard log.
Our cloud provider (Microsoft)
Cloud and AI servicesPatient-facing services run on Microsoft Azure in the UK. Evaluation runs within Europe.

Data

What is and is not kept.

The detail your data protection lead will ask for. More is available on the GDPR and cybersecurity pages.

Words of a conversation
Not stored by OstifyQuestions and answers are not written to Ostify’s storage. Our cloud provider (Microsoft) may keep samples flagged for possible abuse, and does not use them to train AI.
What we do record
Technical detailsSuch as which check responded, the outcome, and how long it took. The record may note which urgent phrase matched.
What you can see
Counts, not conversationsYour usage page shows how many questions were answered, declined or treated as urgent.
Investigating an incident
No transcript to reviewBecause the words are not stored, a specific conversation cannot be replayed. Plan your incident process with this in mind.

Known limits

What it does not do.

No safety system is complete. These are the limits to include in your own risk assessment.

  1. It cannot guarantee detection

    It is designed to detect urgent messages, but some may be missed. Keep your usual routes to urgent help in place.

  2. Outages

    If a safety service stops working, the assistant declines rather than answering without checks. During an outage it may not always show the emergency message.

  3. Live answers

    Live answers are screened for harmful content, but are not individually checked against the source. That detailed check happens during testing.

  4. Very long messages

    Over-long messages are checked for urgency, then the person is asked to shorten them. Parts of very long messages may get fewer checks.

  5. No live alerts to your team

    Your organisation sees urgent events as counts, not as they happen. The assistant does not contact anyone on a patient’s behalf.

  6. Not a medical device

    It provides general information from approved content. It does not diagnose or replace clinical care.

Need more for your assurance process? We can share further evidence under NDA, and you can test the assistant with your own emergency scenarios. Talk to us or read about trust and security.
