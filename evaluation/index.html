Evaluation protocol

How every assistant is tested.

A structured evaluation, assessed by clinicians and reported with its uncertainty. It runs before launch and again as drift analysis every 6 months.

Ask for a sample reportSafety overview

Principles

Four rules behind every evaluation.

These apply to every run.

  • Clinicians decide

    An AI reviewer flags possible problems. It advises, and clinicians make the safety decisions.

  • Findings are never averaged away

    Each clinician’s view is kept separately, and one clinician’s concern is not outvoted.

  • Finished runs are not changed

    A completed run stays as the record of what was found.

  • Uncertainty is shown

    Every count comes with a likely range, because small tests can look more certain than they are.

The protocol

Six steps, from question set to sign-off.

The same process applies to every assistant, sized to its purpose.

  1. Define the purpose

    The assistant’s audience, scope and the things it must never do are recorded first, so answers are judged against them.

  2. Build the question set

    Questions are generated from your content and purpose, then reviewed by your team and locked so later runs are comparable.

  3. Run the assistant

    Each question goes through the same system patients would use, with the same safety checks.

  4. Assess every answer

    The AI reviewer assesses the answers, then clinicians review them and record their decisions.

  5. Review with independent clinicians

    Clinicians other than the test author can be invited to one run. Two or more are advised.

  6. Fix, retest and sign off

    Confirmed problems are fixed, the same questions are asked again, and your organisation decides whether the evidence is good enough.

What is tested

The kinds of question in a test set.

Exact questions and mixes are tailored to each assistant.

Content
Questions your material should answerChecks answers are accurate, complete and faithful to your content.
Everyday life
Practical and lifestyle questionsChecks it stays within your content when a question feels like common sense.
Emergency
Urgent physical and emotional situationsChecks your emergency message is shown when it should be.
Boundary
Clinical decisions it must declineChecks it declines doses, diagnosis, results and prognosis.
Out of scope
Questions your content does not coverChecks it says so rather than guessing.
Adversarial
Attempts to trick itChecks it holds its limits under pressure.
Conversations
Multi-message exchangesChecks behaviour over a conversation, not only single questions.
Repeatability
The same question asked againChecks it behaves the same way each time.

What is measured

Seventeen measures across answers and conversations.

Safety covers six measures, grounding two, and consistency, over-refusal and appropriateness one each. Clinicians then rate selected conversations on six more.

  • Missed emergency

    Did an urgent message get the emergency response?

  • Harmful answer

    Could the answer cause harm, and how seriously?

  • Scope adherence

    Did it stay within its declared limits?

  • Essential information

    Was anything essential left out?

  • Clinically wrong

    Was anything clinically incorrect?

  • False reassurance

    Did it play down something that needed attention?

  • Faithfulness

    Is every claim supported by your content?

  • Citation entailment

    Does the cited passage alone support the claim?

  • Consistency

    Does it behave the same way each time?

  • Over-refusal

    Did it decline something it should have answered?

  • Appropriateness

    Were the empathy and tone right?

Conversation ratings

  • Accuracy

    Was everything it said true?

  • Clarity

    Would the person understand it first time?

  • Relevance

    Did it follow what was actually asked?

  • Completeness

    Did it cover what your content covers?

  • Sources

    Did it stick to the right parts of your content?

  • Usefulness

    Would the person know what to do next?

Reducing bias

How we guard against marking our own work.

No evaluation is free of bias, so these controls are recorded in the report.

  • A separate model

    The AI that marks answers is separate from the one that writes them.

  • Review without the AI

    Clinicians can review without seeing the AI reviewer’s view, and the report shows how many did.

  • Author is identified

    The person who wrote the test set is marked and does not count as an independent reviewer.

  • Verified reviewers

    Invited reviewers sign in with the invited email, so every assessment belongs to a verified person.

What you receive

A report you can put in your safety file.

Available on the assurance plans described on the Assurance page.

  • Headline results

    Unsafe answers and other findings, each with a likely range.

  • Every clinician’s view

    Shown side by side, including what each recorded.

  • Answer-level detail

    Each question, answer and recorded decision.

  • Before and after

    What was fixed, what is still failing, and what is newly failing.

After launch

Evaluation does not stop at launch.

Changes and time can both affect how an assistant behaves.

  • Changes go back through review

    Changing content or key settings means testing and review before patients see it.

  • Drift analysis every 6 months

    We repeat the analysis and send a short report on what has changed.

Limits of the evidence

What an evaluation cannot tell you.

Include these in your own risk assessment.

  • It is a sample

    A test cannot cover every message real people will send.

  • Strong results are not a guarantee

    10 out of 10 could still mean a true rate of about 72%.

  • Test conditions differ

    Real people may behave differently from test questions.

  • Judgement varies

    Clinicians can reasonably disagree, which is why their views stay separate.

What we keep private. We do not publish exact question sets, scoring settings or prompts, because they would help someone get around the safeguards. You can test your assistant with your own scenarios, and further evidence is available under NDA.
